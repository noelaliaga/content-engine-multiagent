# KB — Few-shot analysis exemplar (fictional business)

This example shows the expected LEVEL of specificity. It is not a template to fill with another name; it is a reference for depth of reasoning. The business is fictional and deliberately unnamed, and it belongs to a different niche from the sample client.

## Summarised input (fictional)

A personal-finance coach for freelancers, around 45,000 followers. Bio: "I help freelancers stop fearing their tax return. Course: *Taxes Without Fear*". Observed content: reels about "mistakes I made with my taxes", stories about money anxiety, humour about quarterly filings. Stated goal: sell more course seats without losing current reach.

## What works (evidence, not generic)

The "common mistake + how I fixed it" reels drive most of the account's reach because they combine the **common mistake** pattern with a **real case** (the coach's own mistake, not a hypothetical one). That creates instant identification among freelancers who feel the same anxiety. It is a strong TOFU pattern and should be kept, not replaced.

## Where the leak is (concrete gap, not "could improve")

The feed is roughly 90% emotional TOFU content (anxiety, humour, own mistakes) and close to 0% social proof from students whose anxiety or tax bill actually went down after the course. Gap type: `offer` and `trust` — there is an engaged, emotionally warm audience, but no content shows the method working for third parties. It matters because buying a finance course depends heavily on trust in the method, not only on the emotional connection with the creator.

## Which pattern to adapt (not copy)

"Myth vs reality" is strong in the freelance-finance niche ("no, the tax office is not chasing you for invoicing too little"), but another creator's script must not be copied. Adapt the mechanics — take apart one concrete false belief of this audience, in the coach's direct and light tone — and anchor it to a real benefit of the course.

## What must NOT be copied

The "pay zero tax" promise some accounts in the niche use is a non-guaranteeable outcome and would violate `claim_safety`. The defensible promise is about the method and reduced fear, not a specific tax figure.

## How QA would score it (reasoned scores, not just numbers)

Proposed idea: reel "3 students who stopped dreading their tax return — before/after their first quarter with the method".

- `on_brand`: 5 — consistent with the direct tone and the focus on fear.
- `hook_strength`: 4 — before/after plus social proof, a strong mechanic.
- `pillar_fit`: 5 — fits the missing trust pillar directly.
- `offer_connection`: 5 — connects straight to the course.
- `feasibility`: 3 — depends on recording three student testimonials, not immediate.
- `originality`: 3 — common mechanic in the niche, but reasonable given the gap.
- `claim_safety`: 5 — no figure or guaranteed result, only reduced perceived anxiety.
- `average_score`: 4.29 → verdict `approved`, with `improvement_suggestion`: confirm the students are available before scheduling the piece.
